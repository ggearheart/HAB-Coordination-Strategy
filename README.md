# HAB Coordination Strategy

A conceptual model of how the California State and Regional Water Boards share
responsibility for **harmful algal blooms (HABs)**.

Because a HAB is a *condition* rather than a discharged pollutant, no single
office owns it. This page decomposes that shared jurisdiction into five
coordinated panels — all keyed to the same five-color legend — so a reader can
carry the players from one view to the next:

1. **The five players** — Regions, Division of Water Rights, Division of
   Drinking Water, Division of Water Quality, and the Office of Information
   Management and Analysis (OIMA) — each with the single lever it holds.
2. **The causal chain** — the drivers the Boards can influence (flow, nutrients,
   temperature/residence) → the bloom → the four exposure pathways, with OIMA's
   monitoring as the thread that links driver to bloom.
3. **A lead / support matrix** — exposure pathway against office.
4. **The reservoir convergence** — one node where all five players are activated
   by a single bloom, plus other place archetypes (river beach, Delta, coast).
5. **Freshwater vs. marine** — why the food/seafood pathway has different
   culprit organisms and largely different lead agencies.

## Viewing

Open `index.html` in a browser, or view the published GitHub Pages site.

The page is a single self-contained HTML file (no build step, no dependencies)
and is theme-aware — it follows the viewer's light or dark preference.

## Note

Role assignments are a working characterization of typical responsibilities, not
a legal delineation of authority. Marine seafood-toxin management in California
is led primarily by CDPH, OEHHA, and CDFW.
